# Home surface

Mode: Operate. Users repeatedly open an app, read a notification, or find an app with one hand.

## Direction contract
THESIS: Niagara's vertical favorites and alphabetical navigation, explicitly chosen by the user.
OWN-WORLD: Transparent launcher over the system wallpaper with user-controlled dimming from 0% to 100%, warm white text, native app icons, quiet native sheets. Long-press management uses a deep navy surface and pale blue contour icons matching the supplied reference, an app icon/title header with tap-to-rename, inline additional actions without a rename row, and a separated settings entry. A native 300ms decelerating rise and 180ms downward exit preserve the sheet’s relationship to the bottom edge and honor disabled system animations.
STORY: Open an app directly, drag the alphabet to find another, swipe an app for its pop-up.
FIRST VIEWPORT: An unboxed vertical favorite list without a launcher clock or date; time stays in the Android status bar; a compact Latin alphabet at the lower right with a final # (16sp resting letters, 22dp letter step growing with font scale, and 24dp clearance above the footer; short windows cap letter size to the available step); search and settings at the bottom. Use real installed apps. The curved alphabet tracks every finger movement and highlights the selected section; navigation runs on section changes. The app list recycles visible native rows and jumps directly to the latest selected section. Cyrillic names share the first Latin letter of Android ICU Russian-Latin/BGN transliteration (Яндекс → Yandeks → Y); numbers and initials without Latin transliteration use the final #. Every window shows only populated Latin sections from visible apps, favorites, and #.
FORM: User-pinned Niagara composition; no concept roll replaces an explicit reference. Native implementation, code-led because composition is specified.
FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance

## Favorites order

Home shows “Изменить порядок избранного” below two or more visible favorites. The favorite app's long-press menu opens “Изменить порядок” directly; settings and folder editing also reach the same editor. Drag the 48dp ≡ handle to move apps and folders in one list, or use labeled up/down buttons with keyboard or TalkBack. Changes save immediately without closing the sheet or losing its scroll. Rows wrap long names above a separate action line; at screen widths of at least 600dp, controls join the label line to fit short landscape windows; Close, app selection, and folder creation stay outside the scrolling list. Back and HOME dismiss the editor with the saved order intact.

## Widgets and navigation

The home screen hosts at most one widget. A rightward swipe on wallpaper or other free space opens a separate widget page physically to its left; a leftward swipe on that page returns home. App rows keep their pop-up swipe, hosted widgets keep their touch handling, and the outer 24dp system Back edge is excluded. The footer widget button provides a keyboard/accessibility alternative. The alphabet ends above the measured footer and respects system/IME insets, keeping every footer action reachable at large font sizes.

The widget page inherits the wallpaper, white type, and native controls. Its header contains Home, a wrapping title, a 48dp “Изменить порядок виджетов” action for two or more page widgets, and Add. The same order editor opens from a page widget’s “Изменить порядок” menu action. Drag handles and named arrow buttons reorder with immediate saving; home binding, dimensions and offsets remain attached to each ID. Repeated provider names gain ordinal labels for the current editor session. Widgets form a vertically scrolling list with 28dp side margins and no alphabet overlay; a Home action stays outside the scroll. Empty state explains adding and choosing the home widget. Added widgets have no launcher titles or menu buttons above their content. Hold the widget to open resize, provider configuration when available, move and delete actions. The navy icon/title management sheet opens an editor over the live widget. Four 36dp arrow handles inside 48dp targets resize its upper/lower and left/right edges directly, with no circular reset buttons or slider dialog. The home widget starts 56dp below the inset-safe top (24dp in short windows) and has its own full-width region, independent of the favorites/alphabet margins. Its default width is centered with approximately 36dp margins plus framework/provider padding. Side handles can expand it to the exact inset-safe window width, including widths between grid points, subject to provider max bounds. The alphabet remains above the expanded widget and keeps its Back corridor. Drag the body to move in both axes inside its region and the visible editing area; sizes and movement snap to a fine 12dp grid within provider constraints. Width resizes around its saved center, clamping horizontal offset when necessary; moving the upper edge preserves the lower edge. Sizes save immediately within provider-supported directions/bounds and the available window; Done, an outside tap, Back or HOME end editing. Keyboard arrows and four named accessibility actions move the focused body; range actions resize the same edges. Reset remains a separate management-menu action. Local dimensions and both position offsets survive recreation and portable backup import. Taps and scrolling reach provider content; movement, cancellation and detachment cancel pending holds. Keyboard Menu/Shift+F10 and a named accessibility long-click action open the same controls. Selecting another home widget moves the previous one to the widget page. Back and HOME return home; rotation retains the widget page and its scroll. Page transitions use a short directional slide/fade and honor disabled system animations.

Releasing an active gesture below the letters selects the final section even without a final MOVE. Each completed active gesture emits exactly one CLOCK_TICK on release, identical to section-change feedback, including release on the same section or below the letters. A final hash selection on release shares that tick. After cancellation, a late release does not restart navigation or emit haptic feedback. Android haptic settings remain respected.

Adding a widget opens a sheet with a fixed title/Close, a recycled list of widget labels, application names and 144dp provider previews, plus the shared alphabet control on the right. Provider preview layouts take precedence over images; missing previews are explicitly labeled. The star jumps to the start, Cyrillic labels share Latin sections, and # is last. The list reserves 72dp for the rail; every sheet shows only populated provider sections, with letter size capped to fit short windows. Previews are inert, rows are accessible choices, and browsing allocates no widget IDs. Dismissal cancels resource loading; choosing a row continues the existing binding/configuration flow.

## Widget resize feedback

Every applied width/height change requests one soft SEGMENT_FREQUENT_TICK through AndroidX, including keyboard and accessibility input. Android 14+ uses the native soft effect; Android 12–13 falls back to CLOCK_TICK. Devices unable to produce a suitably soft tick may stay silent on Android 14+. Repeated snapped dimensions, provider/window limits, movement without resizing, release without a new size and cancellation add no tick. Android haptic settings remain respected.
